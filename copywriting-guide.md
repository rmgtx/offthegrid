# Axguard x Kingside -- Copywriting Guide

> Based on strategy call 03/20/2026. This document covers messaging direction for all three touchpoints in the sales funnel: **Flyer > Website/Chatbot > Energy Analyst (on-site).**

---

## Critical Contextual Rules

> These rules override everything else. Every piece of copy must follow them.

1. **ZERO solar language.** Do not discuss, reference, or hint at solar anywhere -- flyer, website, or chatbot first contact. This is a **home battery backup** play. Period.
2. **Website must educate on battery backup.** Every section should reinforce what home battery backup is, why it matters, and what happens when outages hit. The user should walk away understanding the *value of batteries during a crisis* before anything else.
3. **Qualifications go LOW on the website.** Push qualification criteria below the fold, below the education and emotional content. On the **flyer, show NO qualifications at all** -- the flyer's only job is to spark curiosity and drive a QR scan.
4. **Flyer = battery backup + government incentives.** That's it. No solar, no qualifications, no system details. Just crisis-driven battery backup messaging and the federal incentive hook.

---

## Core Messaging Principles

| Principle | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Lead with battery backup** | Every piece of copy starts with home battery backup, storm protection, outage resilience, and grid independence. No solar. |
| **"Extremely gray" positioning** | Copy should feel like a government program update, not a sales pitch. These homeowners get hammered with solar pitches year-round. |
| **Crisis-first storytelling** | Paint the picture of what happens when the grid fails -- then position batteries as the answer. Let the homeowner feel the problem before offering the solution. |
| **Qualification = exclusivity** | Frame qualification questions so the homeowner feels they need to *earn* the program. "Only for homeowners who qualify." But keep this LOW on the page and OFF the flyer entirely. |
| **No installer branding** | Do not mention Legacy or any specific installer. Keep the entity neutral and program-like. |
| **No solar references** | Do not mention solar panels, solar energy, or solar power on any customer-facing material. The product is battery backup. |

## Emotional Levers (Texas-specific)

Use these pain points throughout all copy:

- **2021 freeze** -- families without power for days, frozen pipes, spoiled food
- **Hurricane season** -- Houston-area outages, storm anxiety
- **January 2026 storm scare** -- recent media coverage of potential repeat
- **Grid unreliability** -- ERCOT headlines, rolling blackouts, "it could happen again"

Frame it as: *"When was the last time you lost power? How long? How did it affect your family?"*

## 1. FLYER

**Format:** Postcard size (fits in a door frame)

### Tone
- Government notice / public program announcement
- Clean, authoritative, institutional
- Zero solar language
- Must spark enough curiosity to scan the QR code

### Messaging Direction

**Headline options (choose the angle that tests best):**

- "Texas Homeowners: New Backup Power Incentives Are Now Available"
- "Recent Changes to Federal Home Energy Programs May Affect You"
- "Do You Qualify? Updated Government Incentives for Texas Homeowners"
- "Home Backup Power Program -- Check Your Eligibility"

**Body copy direction:**

- Reference recent legislation / updated federal incentives for home battery backup
- Mention the 30% tax credit for battery backup systems
- Focus on crisis readiness: what happens when the grid goes down and how batteries keep your home running
- Create urgency: "Incentives are subject to change" / "Limited availability"

**CTA:**

- "Scan to check your eligibility" (QR code to website/chatbot)
- Keep it simple. One action. One QR code.

### What NOT to include on the flyer
- The word "solar" or any solar reference
- Any qualification criteria (no credit score, no homeownership questions -- that's the chatbot's job)
- Any installer name (Legacy, etc.)
- Pricing, system details, or technical specs
- Company logos that break the government/program feel

## 2. WEBSITE

**Domain:** .org extension (deciding between `texasbatteryprogram.org` and `texasgridbackup.org`)

### Tone
- Clean, institutional, program-like
- Should feel like an application portal, not a sales page
- Aligned with the "do you qualify?" exclusivity framing
- Think: government benefits site, not landing page

### Messaging Direction

**Hero section:**

- Headline: "Never Lose Power Again" or "Protect Your Home From the Next Outage"
- Subheadline: "Texas homeowners can now get whole-home battery backup through a federally incentivized program"
- CTA button: "Check Your Eligibility" (launches chatbot)

**Supporting sections -- optimized for battery backup education:**

1. **The crisis:** Texas homeowners are vulnerable to power outages. Reference 2021 freeze (4.5M homes, 246 lives lost), hurricanes, grid strain. Use real stats. Make it visceral.
2. **What battery backup does for you:** Explain how home battery systems work during an outage -- lights stay on, fridge stays cold, family stays safe. Emphasize days/weeks of backup power.
3. **Government incentives:** Federal 30% tax credit for battery backup. Energy community bonuses. Texas property tax exemption on added home value. Frame as a limited-time program.
4. **How it works:** Simple 3-step process -- (1) Check eligibility online, (2) Schedule a home assessment with an Energy Analyst, (3) Get battery backup installed.
5. **Qualifications (BOTTOM of page):** Keep this section low. Brief and simple: "Homeowners only. Must meet basic eligibility criteria." Drive them to the chatbot for details.

### What NOT to include on the website
- Any solar language -- no "solar panels," "solar energy," "solar-powered"
- Installer branding
- Pricing or system comparisons
- Anything that feels like a typical solar landing page
- Qualification details above the fold or early in the page flow

## 3. CHATBOT (AI Agent)

### Tone
- Helpful, conversational, but structured
- Program representative -- not a salesperson
- Guides the homeowner through qualification, not a pitch
- Gives enough info to keep the conversation flowing (don't gatekeep too hard behind questions)

### Conversation Flow

```
PHASE 1: HOOK (Backup Power + Pain Points)
    |
    v
PHASE 2: QUALIFY (Must-haves + Nice-to-haves)
    |
    v
PHASE 3: SOLAR REVEAL (Natural, homeowner-led)
    |
    v
PHASE 4: HANDOFF (Schedule Energy Analyst visit)
```

---

### Phase 1: Hook

**Goal:** Build interest around backup power. Surface pain points.

Sample language:
- "Thanks for checking your eligibility! This program helps Texas homeowners protect their homes with backup power."
- "When was the last time you experienced a power outage?"
- "How long were you without power? How did that affect your household?"
- "Many Texas homeowners are taking steps to make sure they're never caught off guard again."

**Key rules:**
- Do NOT mention solar in this phase
- Focus on batteries, backup power, grid protection
- Use their outage experiences to build urgency naturally
- Give enough information to keep them engaged (don't just ask questions with no context)

---

### Phase 2: Qualify

**Goal:** Determine eligibility. Make them feel like they're applying.

**Transition language:**
- "These incentives are only available to homeowners who meet certain criteria. Let me ask a few quick questions to see if you qualify."

**Must-have qualifications (deal-breakers):**

| Question | Requirement |
|---|---|
| "Are you the homeowner?" | Must own the home |
| "Do you currently have solar panels on your home?" | Must NOT already have solar |
| "Is your credit score above 650?" | Credit score > 650 |

**Nice-to-have qualifications (intel for the rep):**

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| "Do you pay more than $80/month for your electric bill?" | Helps size the opportunity |
| "How old is your roof?" (optional for Texas) | Roofs cycle every 7-9 years in TX due to storms, so less of an issue |

**Key rules:**
- Ask qualification questions AFTER providing some context (don't front-load with a form)
- If they don't qualify, exit gracefully
- If they qualify, build excitement: "Great news -- it looks like your home qualifies!"

---

### Phase 3: Deeper Education

**Goal:** Reinforce the value of battery backup. Answer technical questions about how the system works.

Sample language:
- "The backup power system uses advanced battery storage to keep your entire home running during an outage."
- "These batteries charge during off-peak hours and store enough energy to power your home for days."
- "When the grid goes down, your system kicks in automatically -- your family won't even notice."

**Key rules:**
- Keep the focus on batteries, energy storage, and home protection
- Answer questions about how the system charges honestly if asked, but don't lead with it
- The Energy Analyst will cover full system details on-site
- The chatbot's job is to build confidence and schedule the visit, not deliver a technical presentation

---

### Phase 4: Handoff

**Goal:** Schedule the on-site Energy Analyst visit.

**Transition language:**
- "Based on what you've shared, your home qualifies. The next step is to have one of our Energy Analysts come out for a quick visit."
- "They'll take a look at your roof and main electrical panel to confirm everything checks out and go over the final details with you."
- "It typically takes about 20-30 minutes."

**Scheduling:**
- Book via shared Google Calendar
- Calendar invite comes from the neutral program entity (aligned with domain name), NOT from an installer
- Reps have edit access to manage and update

**Key rules:**
- Advertise 20-30 minutes (actual appointment is ~45 min)
- Title the rep as "Energy Analyst"
- Don't promise specific savings or system specs -- that's the rep's job
